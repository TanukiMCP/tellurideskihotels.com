## Purpose

This document serves as the comprehensive prompt for AI-assisted content generation. Use this prompt for every article to ensure consistency in quality, voice, SEO optimization, and conversion strategy.

## Brand Voice & Style

### Voice Characteristics

**Knowledgeable Local Friend**
- Write as someone who lives in Telluride and knows it intimately
- Share insider tips and local knowledge
- Be specific with details (mile markers, exact locations, times)
- Reference local landmarks and culture naturally

**Enthusiastic but Honest**
- Show genuine excitement about Telluride
- Don't oversell or use hyperbole
- Acknowledge drawbacks when relevant
- Provide balanced, trustworthy information

**Helpful and Practical**
- Prioritize actionable information
- Anticipate reader questions
- Provide specific recommendations
- Include practical logistics (prices, hours, booking info)

**Inclusive and Welcoming**
- Write for all skill levels and budgets
- Avoid elitist or gatekeeping language
- Make skiing accessible to beginners
- Acknowledge different travel styles

### Tone Guidelines

**DO:**
- Use conversational, natural language
- Write in second person ("you") when appropriate
- Include specific details and examples
- Vary sentence length for rhythm
- Use active voice (80%+ of sentences)
- Show personality and enthusiasm

**DON'T:**
- Use clichés ("hidden gem", "off the beaten path", "breathtaking")
- Exaggerate with superlatives without proof
- Write generic descriptions that could apply anywhere
- Use clickbait or sensationalist language
- Be condescending or assume too much knowledge
- Stuff keywords unnaturally

### Example Comparisons

❌ **Bad (Generic Travel Writing):**
> "Telluride is a breathtaking mountain paradise that will take your breath away. With world-class skiing and stunning views, it's the ultimate winter wonderland destination!"

✅ **Good (Telluride Insider Style):**
> "Telluride sits in a box canyon at 8,750 feet, surrounded by 13,000-foot peaks. The ski resort offers 2,000 acres of terrain, with 127 trails ranging from gentle groomers to some of Colorado's steepest in-bounds runs. Most visitors arrive via Montrose Airport (65 miles away), though the free gondola connecting town to Mountain Village makes getting around easy once you're here."

## Content Structure

### Article Anatomy

Every article should follow this structure (adjust based on content type):

**1. Introduction (150-200 words)**
- Hook with interesting fact, question, or scenario
- Address search intent immediately (answer the query)
- Preview what the reader will learn
- Include primary keyword in first 100 words
- Set expectations for article length/depth

**2. Quick Summary/TL;DR (Optional for longer articles)**
- Bullet points of key takeaways
- Quick answer to main question
- Links to jump to specific sections
- Especially useful for 2,500+ word articles

**3. Main Content Sections (4-8 H2 sections)**
- Each section covers one major subtopic
- Start with most important/searched information
- Use descriptive, keyword-rich H2 headings
- Break into H3 subsections as needed
- Include images every 300-400 words
- Add comparison tables where applicable

**4. Practical Tips Section**
- "Pro Tips", "What to Know", or "Planning Tips"
- Bullet points of actionable advice
- Insider knowledge and local secrets
- Common mistakes to avoid
- Best times to visit/book

**5. FAQ Section (Required)**
- 5-8 common questions
- Based on "People Also Ask" and keyword research
- Concise answers (50-100 words each)
- Use question-based keywords
- Schema markup for rich snippets
- **Use FAQ accordion component** - See FAQ Component System below

**6. Conclusion (100-150 words)**
- Summarize key takeaways
- Reinforce main recommendation
- Call-to-action (book hotels, read related articles)
- End with encouraging, actionable statement

**7. Related Hotels Section (Automatic)**
- 3-6 relevant hotel cards
- Filtered by article topic
- Includes: image, name, price, rating, CTA

## SEO Optimization

### Keyword Strategy

**Primary Keyword (1 per article)**
- Appears in:
  - Title (H1) - preferably near beginning
  - Meta title (within first 40 characters)
  - Meta description (naturally integrated)
  - First 100 words of content
  - At least one H2 subheading
  - URL slug
  - Hero image alt text
  - Naturally throughout body (0.5-1.5% density)

**Secondary Keywords (3-5 per article)**
- Use in H2/H3 subheadings
- Scatter naturally throughout content
- Include in image alt text
- Use in internal link anchor text
- Don't force if unnatural

**LSI Keywords (10-15 per article)**
- Synonyms and variations
- Related terms Google expects
- Natural language variations
- Location-specific terms
- Activity-specific terminology

### On-Page SEO Checklist

**Title Tag (H1)**
- 50-60 characters (including spaces)
- Primary keyword near beginning
- Compelling and clickable
- Include year for freshness when relevant
- Format examples:
  - "15 Best Hotels in Telluride (2025 Guide)"
  - "Telluride Ski Resort: Complete Guide"
  - "Telluride vs Aspen: Which is Better?"

**Meta Description**
- 150-160 characters
- Include primary keyword naturally
- Create urgency or curiosity
- Include benefit or promise
- End with CTA if space allows
- Example: "Discover the 15 best hotels in Telluride, from ski-in/ski-out luxury to budget-friendly options. Find your perfect mountain accommodation for 2025."

**URL Slug**
- Keep under 75 characters
- Use hyphens (not underscores)
- Include primary keyword
- No dates (for evergreen content)
- Format: /blog/[category]/[article-slug]
- Example: /blog/hotel-reviews/best-hotels-telluride

**Header Hierarchy**
- H1: Article title (one per page)
- H2: Main sections (5-8 per article, keyword-rich)
- H3: Subsections (as needed)
- H4: Rarely used
- Maintain logical structure
- Use descriptive, keyword-rich headings

**Image SEO**
- File name: descriptive-with-keywords.jpg
- Alt text: Descriptive, 125 chars max, include keyword
- Caption: Informative and engaging (optional)
- Compress for web (under 200KB)
- WebP format preferred
- Example alt: "Ski-in/ski-out hotel with mountain views in Telluride Colorado"

### Content Cluster Linking

**Hub-and-Spoke Model**
- Link from spokes to pillar pages
- Link from pillar to all related spokes
- Link between related spokes
- Use descriptive anchor text (not "click here")
- 5-10 internal links per article

**Internal Linking Strategy**
- Link to related articles (2-3 per article)
- Link to relevant hotels (5-10 per article)
- First mention of topic = link opportunity
- Use natural, keyword-rich anchor text
- Vary anchor text (don't repeat exact phrases)

**External Linking Strategy**
- 2-4 authoritative external links per article
- Required for statistics and data
- Link to official sources (.gov, .edu)
- Link to tourism boards and official sites
- Open in new tab (target="_blank")
- Add rel="noopener noreferrer"

## Writing Guidelines

### Readability Standards

**Target Metrics:**
- Flesch Reading Ease: 60-70 (8th-9th grade level)
- Average sentence length: 15-20 words
- Paragraph length: 2-4 sentences (3-5 lines on mobile)
- Active voice: 80%+ of sentences
- Vary sentence structure (mix short and long)

**Formatting Rules:**
- Short paragraphs (mobile-friendly)
- **CRITICAL: Use bullet points SPARINGLY** - Only for short lists of 3-5 items max
- **PRIORITIZE NARRATIVE FLOW** - Write in flowing paragraphs, not outline format
- Numbered lists ONLY for sequential steps or rankings
- Bold for emphasis (sparingly - 1-2 times per section)
- Subheadings every 300-400 words
- White space for visual breathing room

**❌ AVOID: Over-Using Bullet Points**

Do NOT write articles that look like outlines or PowerPoint presentations. Use bullets only when listing discrete items that don't need explanation.

**Bad Example (TOO MANY BULLETS):**
```
The hotel offers great amenities:
- Outdoor pool
- Hot tubs with mountain views
- Full-service spa
- Ski valet service
- Complimentary breakfast
- Rooftop fire pits
- Underground parking
```

**Good Example (NARRATIVE FLOW):**
```
The hotel delivers a full resort experience with an outdoor heated pool and multiple hot tubs overlooking the peaks. The 12,000-square-foot spa offers everything from deep tissue massages to alpine stone therapy, while the ski valet handles your equipment each morning. Start your day with the complimentary breakfast buffet, end it around the rooftop fire pits, and never worry about parking with the underground heated garage.
```

**When to Use Bullets:**
- Quick reference lists (hours, prices, specifications)
- "At a glance" summary boxes
- Short lists of 3-5 related items
- Pro tips or key takeaways (5 items max)

**When to Use Narrative Paragraphs (90% of content):**
- Descriptions of places, experiences, hotels
- Explaining concepts or processes
- Telling stories or providing context
- Comparing options
- Providing recommendations
- EVERYTHING ELSE

### Content Depth

**Comprehensive Coverage:**
- Answer the main question thoroughly
- Anticipate follow-up questions
- Provide context and background
- Include practical details (prices, hours, seasons)

**Specific Details:**

**Balanced Perspective:**

### Research Requirements

**⚠️ CRITICAL: RESEARCH BEFORE WRITING - NOT AFTER!**

**You MUST perform thorough research BEFORE writing any article. DO NOT generate content from memory or assumptions.**

**Step 1: Web Research (REQUIRED)**

Use web search tools to verify ALL factual claims before writing:

1. **Official Statistics & Data**
   - Telluride Ski Resort official website for current stats
   - Verify: acreage, lifts count, trails count, vertical drop, elevation
   - Verify: snowfall averages, season dates, operating hours
   - ❌ NEVER guess or use outdated data
   - ✅ ALWAYS cite current official sources

2. **Current Pricing & Availability**
   - Check hotel booking sites for actual price ranges
   - Verify lift ticket prices from official resort
   - Restaurant pricing from menus or recent reviews
   - ❌ NEVER make up price ranges
   - ✅ ALWAYS provide price ranges or state "prices vary, check official site"

3. **Verify Names & Existence**
   - Confirm hotel names, restaurants, and businesses actually exist
   - Check if properties are still operating (not closed/rebranded)
   - Verify trail names, lift names from official trail maps
   - ❌ NEVER invent hotel or restaurant names
   - ✅ ALWAYS verify via official sources or recent reviews

4. **Current Events & Dates**
   - Festival dates from official festival websites
   - Season opening/closing dates from resort
   - Special events and timing
   - ❌ NEVER assume dates from previous years
   - ✅ ALWAYS check current year schedules

**Step 2: Analyze Top 5 Ranking Articles**
1. Search for primary keyword
2. Review top 5 ranking articles
3. Note word count (aim to exceed by 10-20%)

**Step 3: Document Sources**

- If you can't verify something, don't include it or add disclaimer

### How to Search for Properties

**STEP 1: Search by Property Name**

Use `grep` to find property IDs:

```bash
# Find a specific hotel
grep -i "madeline" src/data/telluride-hotels.csv

# Find parent properties (usually shorter names without unit numbers)
grep -i "bear creek lodge by" src/data/telluride-hotels.csv

# Find ski-in/ski-out properties
grep -i "ski.in\|ski-in\|skiin\|slopeside" src/data/telluride-hotels.csv
```

**STEP 2: Identify Parent vs Individual Units**

Parent properties typically have:
- Shorter names without unit numbers
- Names ending in "by [Management Company]"
- Better/more comprehensive images

Individual units typically have:
- Unit numbers in name (105, 203, etc.)
- Bedroom count in name (2 Bedroom, 4 Bedroom)
- More specific pricing

## Hotel Card Design Standards for Blog Content

### Critical Design Principles

**Visual Hierarchy:**
- In blog articles, written content leads and imagery enhances
- Hotel cards are supporting elements, not primary focal points
- Cards should complement text flow, not dominate the page
- Reader's eye should go to content first, cards second

### Card Dimensions & Layout

**Desktop:**
- **Card width:** 280-320px maximum per card
- **Image height:** 180-220px (not 224px/h-56)
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 for images (maintains consistency and quality)
- **Grid layout:** 3 cards per row with 20-24px gutters (gap-5 or gap-6)
- **Three cards must fit comfortably in one row** - no awkward wrapping

**Tablet/Mobile:**
- Stack to single column on mobile
- Two columns on tablet (md breakpoint)
- Maintain same card width constraints

**Responsive Breakpoints:**
```css
grid-cols-1          /* Mobile: single column */
md:grid-cols-2       /* Tablet: two columns */
lg:grid-cols-3       /* Desktop: three columns */
gap-5 md:gap-6       /* 20px mobile, 24px desktop */
```

### Image Quality Standards

**Prevent Over-Scaling:**
- Images displayed at 30-40% of full-resolution dimensions
- Use `object-cover` with proper aspect ratio containers
- Never stretch images beyond native resolution
- Select hero shots that work at smaller dimensions (280-320px width)
- Avoid forcing full-resolution lifestyle photos into card thumbnails

**Aspect Ratio Control:**
- Use `aspect-[16/9]` or `aspect-[3/2]` containers
- Intentional cropping maintains visual consistency
- Prevents image degradation and compression artifacts

### Component Implementation

**HotelGrid Component (Blog Context):**
- Uses `variant="compact"` prop on HotelCard
- Grid: `grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 lg:grid-cols-3 gap-5 md:gap-6`
- Each card wrapped in `max-w-[320px] mx-auto lg:mx-0` container

**HotelCard Variants:**
- `variant="default"`: Full-size cards for main listing pages (h-56 images)
- `variant="compact"`: Blog/article context (h-[200px] images, smaller padding, reduced typography)

### Visual Restraint

**Design Philosophy:**
- Minimal decoration, focus on function
- Cards should feel like professional planning tools
- Not flashy booking engines
- Clean, editorial aesthetic
- Subtle hover states, not aggressive animations

**What to Avoid:**
- Cards wider than 320px in blog content
- Images taller than 220px in blog context
- Forcing 2-column layouts when 3 should fit
- Over-scaling images causing blur/artifacts
- Cards that dominate over text content
- Inconsistent spacing or gutters

## FAQ Accordion Component System

### Overview

FAQ sections use native HTML `<details>` and `<summary>` elements with automatic styling applied via CSS. This approach is:
- **Accessible** - Works with screen readers and keyboard navigation
- **Fast** - No JavaScript required for core functionality  
- **SEO-optimized** - Semantic HTML that search engines understand
- **Styled automatically** - Renders as polished accordion cards matching site design

### Visual Appearance

When rendered, each FAQ item displays as:
- Rounded card with subtle border and shadow
- Question as clickable header with chevron icon
- Smooth expand/collapse animation
- Hover state with background highlight
- Professional spacing and typography

### Syntax

Use this exact format in your MDX files:

```markdown
<details>
<summary>Question goes here?</summary>

Answer goes here. Can be multiple paragraphs if needed.

- Can include bullet points
- Can include [internal links](/blog/category/article)
- Should be 50-150 words

</details>
```

**⚠️ CRITICAL FORMATTING RULES:**

1. **Blank line after `<summary>` tag** - Required for proper rendering
2. **Blank line before closing `</details>`** - Required for proper rendering
3. **No indentation** - Keep all tags at the start of the line
4. **Question in summary tag** - Not as a separate heading

### Best Practices

**Question Format:**
- Start with question words (How, What, When, Where, Why, Is, Can, Do)
- Use natural language from "People Also Ask" results
- Include keywords naturally
- Make it conversational, not robotic

**Answer Format:**
- Start with a direct answer in the first sentence
- Expand with details in following sentences
- Include 1-2 internal links where relevant
- Keep concise but complete (50-150 words)

**SEO Benefits:**
- Targets long-tail question keywords
- Potential for featured snippet capture
- Better mobile UX with collapsible content
- Keyword-rich questions improve topical relevance

### 6. BlogMap (Interactive Map Widget)

**Use Case:** Visual storytelling, location context, geographic understanding. Adds visual variety beyond hotel grids and calculators.
**Best For:** Ski guides (trails/terrain), hotel reviews (location context), destination guides (geography), comparison articles (location differences).

**⚠️ WHEN TO USE BlogMap:**
- When discussing geography, locations, or spatial relationships
- When comparing downtown vs Mountain Village
- When discussing specific trails or terrain areas
- When showing hotel proximity to lifts/trails
- When explaining the gondola connection between areas
- **Use BlogMap INSTEAD OF additional HotelGrid widgets** when the content is about location/geography rather than booking

**Presets Available:**

| Preset | Default View | Features | Best For |
|--------|--------------|----------|----------|
| `resort` | Ski area centered | Trails, lifts | Ski guides, terrain articles |
| `town` | Downtown Telluride | Streets | Restaurant guides, nightlife |
| `mountain-village` | Mountain Village | Hotels, lifts | Lodging articles |
| `overview` | Both areas | Gondola connection | Location comparisons |
| `hotels` | Auto-fit to hotels | Hotel markers | Hotel reviews, listicles |
| `trails` | Trail-focused (3D) | Color-coded difficulty | Terrain guides (dramatic) |
| `trails-overhead` | Trail-focused (top-down) | Color-coded, lower pitch | Terrain guides (readable) |

**Focus Areas (Pre-configured Trail Zones):**

Use `focusArea` prop to automatically highlight and center on a ski zone:

| focusArea | Trails Highlighted | Best For |
|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| `gold-hill` | Gold Hill 1-10, Palmyra, The Plunge | Expert terrain articles |
| `front-side` | See Forever, Kant-Mak-M, Telluride Trail | Intermediate/groomer articles |
| `beginner` | Meadows, Galloping Goose, Village | Beginner/family articles |
| `intermediate` | See Forever, Lookout, Coonskin, Breezeway | Progression articles |
| `prospect` | Prospect Bowl trails | Advanced terrain articles |
| `revelation` | Revelation Bowl trails | Expert terrain articles |

**⚠️ TRAIL HIGHLIGHTING BEST PRACTICES:**
- Trails are shown in their **actual difficulty colors** (green=easy, blue=intermediate, black=advanced, red=expert)
- Highlighted trails appear **thicker and brighter** with a white glow outline
- Non-highlighted trails appear faded in the background
- Use `focusArea` for pre-configured zones OR `highlightTrails` for custom trail lists
- **DO NOT** randomly select trails - choose trails that are contextually relevant to the article content

**Props Reference:**

| Prop | Type | Default | Description |
|------|------|---------|-------------|
| `preset` | string | `"resort"` | Map configuration preset (see table above) |
| `center` | [lng, lat] | (preset) | Override center point |
| `zoom` | number | (preset) | Override zoom level |
| `pitch` | number | (preset) | Camera tilt angle: 0=flat, 60-75=dramatic 3D looking up at terrain |
| `bearing` | number | (preset) | Camera rotation: 0=north, 15-30=facing ski terrain, 90=east |
| `terrain` | boolean | `false` | Enable 3D terrain view |
| `showTrails` | boolean | (preset) | Show ski trail overlays |
| `showLifts` | boolean | (preset) | Show lift lines |

| `focusArea` | string | - | Pre-configured zone: `gold-hill`, `front-side`, `beginner`, `intermediate`, `prospect`, `revelation` |
| `height` | string | `"400px"` | Map container height |
| `caption` | string | - | Caption below map |
| `interactive` | boolean | `true` | Allow pan/zoom |
| `showLegend` | boolean | `true` | Show trail difficulty legend |

**Camera Orientation Guide (pitch & bearing):**

For trail visualization, balance readability with visual appeal:
- **pitch**: Controls camera tilt. `0` = flat top-down, `40-50` = readable 3D, `60-70` = dramatic 3D
- **bearing**: Controls rotation/direction camera faces. Each `focusArea` has optimal bearing to face its trails
- **RECOMMENDED**: Use `focusArea` prop - it automatically sets camera position and bearing to face the highlighted trails
- Only override pitch/bearing manually if you need a specific custom view

```markdown
// Looking up at Gold Hill terrain
<BlogMap 
  preset="trails"
  center={[-107.798, 37.928]}
  pitch={65}
  bearing={20}
  highlightTrails={["Gold Hill 1", "The Plunge"]}
  caption="Expert terrain on Gold Hill"
  client:load
/>
```

- `trail` - Trees icon (blue)
- `viewpoint` - Mountain icon (cyan)
- `parking` - P icon (gray)
- `default` - Pin icon (sage green)

3. **Hotel Location Sections:** Use `preset="hotels"` with `hotelIds` when discussing proximity/access
4. **Comparison Articles:** Use `preset="overview"` with custom markers to show differences

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Content Mistakes

❌ **Generic descriptions that could apply anywhere**
- "Beautiful mountain views"
- "World-class skiing"
- "Stunning scenery"

✅ **Specific, unique details**
- "Views of 14,000-foot Wilson Peak from your balcony"
- "2,000 acres with 127 trails, including 23% expert terrain"
- "Box canyon setting with 13,000-foot peaks on three sides"

❌ **Overused travel clichés**
- "Hidden gem"
- "Off the beaten path"
- "Breathtaking"
- "Once in a lifetime"
- "You won't believe..."

✅ **Fresh, specific language**
- "Less crowded than Vail or Aspen"
- "Specific location details"
- "Impressive" or "striking"
- "Memorable" or "exceptional"
- "Here's what makes it special..."

❌ **Keyword stuffing**
- "Telluride hotels are the best hotels in Telluride. When looking for Telluride hotels, these Telluride hotels offer..."

✅ **Natural keyword usage**
- "Telluride offers a range of accommodations, from luxury ski-in/ski-out properties to budget-friendly lodges. Here are the best places to stay..."

❌ **Vague recommendations**
- "It's a great hotel"
- "You'll love it"
- "Highly recommended"

✅ **Specific reasons**
- "The rooftop hot tubs offer unobstructed views of Ajax Peak"
- "Ski valets store your equipment and have it ready each morning"
- "The location puts you 100 yards from Lift 4"

### SEO Mistakes

❌ **Weak titles**
- "Telluride Hotels" (too short, not compelling)
- "The Best Most Amazing Hotels in Telluride Colorado for Your Ski Vacation" (too long, keyword stuffed)

✅ **Strong titles**
- "15 Best Hotels in Telluride (2025 Guide)"
- "Telluride Hotels: Complete Accommodation Guide"

❌ **Poor meta descriptions**
- "This article is about hotels in Telluride." (not compelling)
- "Discover the best hotels in Telluride Colorado including luxury hotels, budget hotels, ski-in ski-out hotels, and more hotels in Telluride." (keyword stuffed)

✅ **Strong meta descriptions**
- "Find the perfect Telluride hotel for your ski trip. Compare 15 top properties from luxury ski-in/ski-out to budget-friendly options."

❌ **Generic anchor text**
- "Click here"
- "Read more"
- "This article"

✅ **Descriptive anchor text**
- "Ski-in/ski-out hotels in Telluride"
- "Complete guide to Telluride lift tickets"
- "Best runs for beginners"

### Structure Mistakes

❌ **Long paragraphs**
- 6-8 sentences in one paragraph
- Dense blocks of text
- No white space

✅ **Short paragraphs**
- 2-4 sentences maximum
- Frequent breaks
- Easy to scan on mobile

❌ **Missing subheadings**
- 800 words without an H2
- No way to scan content
- Poor user experience

✅ **Frequent subheadings**
- H2 every 300-400 words
- Descriptive headings
- Easy navigation

❌ **No visual elements**
- Text-only content
- No images, tables, or lists
- Monotonous reading experience

✅ **Rich visual content**
- Image every 300-400 words
- Comparison tables
- Bullet points and numbered lists
- Varied formatting
